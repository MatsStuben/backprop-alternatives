\section{Conclusion}

This thesis investigated perturbation-based learning as a biologically motivated alternative to backpropagation. The work was organized around three research questions, which are answered directly below.

\textbf{What is the current state of perturbation-based learning?}  
The thesis concludes that perturbation-based learning is an established but still incomplete research direction. Several papers have shown that neural networks can be trained using perturbation-based updates, and the literature contains many variants that differ in where noise is injected, how the learning signal is formed, and how the update is scaled. In Chapter~2, we identified 21 different update rules across 9 papers and organized them in a common notation. This showed that the literature has gradually moved from earlier finite-difference formulations toward score-function-style estimators, which are the versions most relevant to this thesis. The two central families of perturbation methods are weight perturbation, where noise is applied directly to the parameters, and node perturbation, where noise is applied to neural activity or pre-activations. However, despite the existence of many relevant papers, the relationship between WP and NP remains insufficiently resolved in nonlinear multilayer networks. The current state of the literature is therefore that perturbation-based learning is a biologically motivated learning principle that can train neural networks, but that the comparison between its two most important variants has remained incomplete.

\textbf{How can we improve the current state of perturbation-based learning?}  
The strongest contribution of the thesis is the answer to this question. Chapter~2 identified an important gap in the literature: although node perturbation is often motivated as more efficient than weight perturbation, the relationship between WP and NP was not fully explained at the estimator level in nonlinear multilayer networks. Chapter~3 addressed this gap by applying the logic of the local reparameterization trick to perturbation-based learning. This led to the derivation of input-scaled node perturbation, a node-level method that samples the same pre-activation noise distribution induced by Gaussian WP. We then showed that WP and IS-NP estimate the same perturbation-smoothed objective, but that IS-NP has lower or equal coordinate-wise variance. The empirical results support the practical relevance of this result: WP has much higher estimator variance than the NP methods and performs worse as task complexity increases. In this sense, the thesis improves the current theoretical understanding of perturbation-based learning by showing why node-level perturbation should generally be preferred over weight-level perturbation when both are viable. At the same time, the theoretical analysis also produced a new method. IS-NP not only improves upon WP in the matched-variance sense, but also performs better than vanilla NP and fan-in-scaled NP in the most challenging experiments. The thesis therefore improves the current state of perturbation-based learning both by clarifying the WP--NP relationship and by deriving a practically promising new perturbation method.

\textbf{What is the practical capability of perturbation-based learning compared with backpropagation?}  
The empirical results show that perturbation-based learning is capable of training nontrivial multilayer networks, including meaningful performance on MNIST classification using an MLP. However, the methods remain clearly limited compared with backpropagation. The gap is small on the simplest task, becomes more visible on California housing and MNIST, and is large on CIFAR-10, where the perturbation methods do not approach the speed or final performance of backpropagation. This suggests that current perturbation-based methods are not sufficient to train state-of-the-art models or solve highly complex supervised learning problems competitively. Their significance is instead that they demonstrate that biologically motivated learning rules based on stochastic perturbations and scalar feedback can support genuine learning. This is consistent with the biological motivation from Chapter~2: three-factor learning is not a complete theory of learning in the brain, but one plausible mechanism among several. In the same way, perturbation-based learning is unlikely to be the only learning rule needed in future biologically inspired AI systems, but it may serve as one useful building block. Substantial improvements, or combinations with other learning mechanisms, are therefore needed before such methods can become competitive alternatives to exact gradient-based training.

Overall, the thesis shows that perturbation-based learning should not be viewed as a direct competitor to backpropagation in standard neural networks, but as a biologically motivated learning principle with clear theoretical structure and limited but real practical capability. The field is still far from fully explored, and the results suggest that further theoretical and algorithmic development could substantially improve these methods. This thesis pushes in that direction by clarifying that node-level perturbation is the more promising path than weight-level perturbation when both are viable, and by turning that insight into IS-NP: a new perturbation method that is theoretically justified and empirically stronger than existing perturbation variants in the settings studied here.
