\section*{Preface}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Preface}

This master’s thesis concludes my five years in the Master’s programme in Cybernetics and Robotics at the Department of Engineering Cybernetics at the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU).

The motivation for this thesis came from my specialization project, where I first worked on biologically plausible alternatives to backpropagation and implemented perturbation-based learning methods. That work showed that perturbation methods were capable of training neural networks on supervised learning tasks, and also made it clear that this area was far from overexplored in the literature. This became the starting point for the present thesis. Although the objectives and contributions of this thesis are substantially different, both works build on the same underlying motivation: understanding how learning in the brain can inspire alternatives to backpropagation in artificial neural networks. Parts of the background material are therefore based on the specialization project.

I would like to express my sincere gratitude to my supervisor, Bjarne André Grimstad, for his guidance, feedback, and insightful discussions throughout the work on this thesis. His support has been highly valuable, both in shaping the direction of the project and in improving the clarity of the final work.

I would also like to thank my family and friends for their support throughout my studies. Finally, I want to thank NTNU, the Department of Engineering Cybernetics, and the city of Trondheim for five memorable years.

I would also like to thank Erling Braut Haaland for his careful review of the text, clinical finishing in the comment margins, and for reminding me that even a thesis occasionally needs to be put in the back of the net.

Trondheim, June 2026

Mats Wiig Martinussen
