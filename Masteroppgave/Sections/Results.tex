\subsection{Sinus Regression}

Figure~\ref{fig:sinus_results} and Table~\ref{tab:sinus_results_summary} show that all methods were able to learn the sinus regression task well. The node perturbation methods converged to training and test losses close to the backpropagation baseline, with IS-NP and fan-in NP reaching nearly identical loss values. WP ended at a slightly higher loss level, but remained close to the other perturbation methods. IS-NP reached the convergence criterion at approximately the same epoch as backpropagation, while fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP converged somewhat later. The standard deviations reported for the best loss and score values are small, indicating little variation across seeds. The fan-in NP curves show somewhat larger fluctuations during training, but without signs of sustained instability. Overall, all perturbation methods worked well as learning algorithms on this task.

The estimator diagnostics show a clearer separation between WP and the node perturbation methods. The three node perturbation methods obtained similar cosine similarity and variance values, with only small differences between them. Vanilla NP had the highest cosine similarity, followed by fan-in NP and IS-NP, while the variance followed the same ordering, meaning that the methods with slightly higher cosine similarity also had slightly higher estimator variance. The differences between the node perturbation methods are therefore small and do not give a clear distinction between them. WP had both lower cosine similarity and higher estimator variance than all node perturbation methods. The reported standard deviations are small for both metrics, indicating that these diagnostic results are consistent across seeds.

\subsection{California Housing Regression}

Figure~\ref{fig:california_housing_results} and Table~\ref{tab:california_housing_results_summary} show that all methods were able to learn meaningful predictors on the California housing task, although the perturbation methods converged to higher training and test loss levels than backpropagation. Among the perturbation methods, the three node perturbation methods obtained very similar training and test performance, with no clear separation between them. WP reached the convergence criterion earliest among the perturbation methods, but ended with the highest training and test loss. The standard deviations reported for the best loss and score values are small for BP, IS-NP, vanilla NP, and WP, while fan-in NP shows somewhat larger variation across seeds.

The estimator diagnostics show a clear separation between WP and the node perturbation methods. Vanilla NP obtained the highest cosine similarity, followed closely by IS-NP, while fan-in NP was lower than both. WP had a substantially lower cosine similarity than all node perturbation methods. The variance ranking follows a similar pattern: IS-NP and vanilla NP obtained the lowest estimator variance values, fan-in NP was higher, and WP had the highest variance. The clearest distinction is therefore between WP and the node perturbation methods, but fan-in NP is also separated from IS-NP and vanilla NP on both diagnostic metrics.

\subsection{MNIST Classification}

Figure~\ref{fig:mnist_results} and Table~\ref{tab:mnist_results_summary} show that MNIST is the first task where the differences between the learning rules become clearly visible in both training and test performance. All methods learn meaningful representations, with test accuracies well above the 10\% chance level, but they do not learn equally well or equally fast. The ordering is clear across both loss and accuracy: backpropagation performs best, followed by IS-NP, fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. Backpropagation reaches the highest test accuracy at 98.5\%, while IS-NP obtains the best perturbation-based performance with a test accuracy of 96.2\%. Fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP reach progressively lower accuracy and higher test loss. The WP loss curve also begins to increase toward the end of training, suggesting that the noisier update estimates are close to becoming unstable in this setting.

The estimator diagnostics follow the same overall ordering as the performance metrics. IS-NP obtains the highest cosine similarity and the lowest estimator variance among the perturbation methods, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. The difference between IS-NP and fan-in NP is not large, especially for cosine similarity, but IS-NP is still best on both diagnostic metrics. Vanilla NP is clearly weaker than the two scaled node perturbation methods, with lower cosine similarity and higher variance. WP is separated most strongly from the other perturbation methods, with substantially lower cosine similarity and much higher estimator variance.

\subsection{CIFAR-10 Classification}

Figure~\ref{fig:cifar10_results} and Table~\ref{tab:cifar10_results_summary} show that the differences between methods remain clear on the most difficult task. The gap between backpropagation and the perturbation methods is larger on CIFAR-10 than on MNIST. The perturbation methods still learn useful structure and obtain test accuracies well above the 10\% chance level, with IS-NP reaching the strongest perturbation-based performance at 36.2\%. However, all perturbation methods remain far behind backpropagation, which reaches a test accuracy of 55.8\%. Although the four perturbation-method curves appear close in absolute terms, the total loss decrease on CIFAR-10 is modest, so these differences are still meaningful and consistent. The perturbation methods follow the same ordering as on MNIST: IS-NP performs best, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP.

The estimator diagnostics follow the same ordering as the performance metrics. IS-NP obtains the highest cosine similarity and the lowest estimator variance among the perturbation methods, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. As on MNIST, the difference between IS-NP and fan-in NP is relatively small, especially compared with the gap to vanilla NP and WP, but IS-NP remains best on both diagnostic metrics. Vanilla NP is weaker than the two scaled node perturbation methods, with lower cosine similarity and higher variance. WP is again separated most strongly from the other perturbation methods, with substantially lower cosine similarity and much higher estimator variance.

\subsection{Scaled Input Sensitivity}

Figure~\ref{fig:scaled_input_results} shows the effect of scaling the sinus input range from $[-1,1]$ to $[-5,5]$. For IS-NP and WP, the input scaling has little effect on either cosine similarity or estimator variance. In contrast, vanilla NP and fan-in NP both show substantially lower cosine similarity and higher estimator variance after the input range is scaled.
