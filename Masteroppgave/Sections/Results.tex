\section{Results}
\label{ch:results}

For each learning problem, the main summary table reports the numerical performance and diagnostic metrics. In addition, a ranking table is included for the perturbation methods only. Each perturbation method is assigned a rank from 1 to 4 for each metric, where 1 is best. Lower values are better for loss, convergence epoch, and estimator variance, while higher values are better for score and cosine similarity. The overall rank is the average across the metric-specific ranks, so lower overall scores indicate better performance. After the four task-specific rankings, an aggregate ranking is also reported by averaging the same metric-specific ranks across all four tasks.

\subsection{Sinus Regression}

Figure~\ref{fig:sinus_results} and Table~\ref{tab:sinus_results_summary} show that all methods were able to learn the sinus regression task well. The node perturbation methods converged to training and test losses close to the backpropagation baseline, with IS-NP and fan-in NP reaching nearly identical loss values. WP ended at a slightly higher loss level, but remained close to the other perturbation methods. IS-NP reached the convergence criterion at approximately the same epoch as backpropagation, while fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP converged somewhat later. The standard deviations reported for the best loss and score values are small, indicating little variation across seeds. The fan-in NP curves show somewhat larger fluctuations during training, but without signs of sustained instability. Overall, all perturbation methods worked well as learning algorithms on this task.

The estimator diagnostics show a clearer separation between WP and the node perturbation methods. The three node perturbation methods obtained similar cosine similarity and variance values, with only small differences between them. Vanilla NP had the highest cosine similarity, followed by fan-in NP and IS-NP, while the variance followed the same ordering, meaning that the methods with slightly higher cosine similarity also had slightly higher estimator variance. The differences between the node perturbation methods are therefore small and do not give a clear distinction between them. WP had both lower cosine similarity and higher estimator variance than all node perturbation methods. The reported standard deviations are small for both metrics, indicating that these diagnostic results are consistent across seeds.

The metric-wise ranking in Table~\ref{tab:sinus_results_ranking} gives the lowest average rank to IS-NP, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. The clearest distinction is between WP and the node perturbation methods, with WP receiving the lowest rank on every reported metric. Among the three node perturbation methods, the best-ranked method varies across individual metrics, especially for cosine similarity and variance. However, the average ranks separate the methods relatively clearly in the order IS-NP, fan-in NP, and vanilla NP.

\subsection{California Housing Regression}

Figure~\ref{fig:california_housing_results} and Table~\ref{tab:california_housing_results_summary} show that all methods were able to learn meaningful predictors on the California housing task, although the perturbation methods converged to higher training and test loss levels than backpropagation. Among the perturbation methods, the three node perturbation methods obtained very similar training and test performance, with no clear separation between them. WP reached the convergence criterion earliest among the perturbation methods, but ended with the highest training and test loss. The standard deviations reported for the best loss and score values are small for BP, IS-NP, vanilla NP, and WP, while fan-in NP shows somewhat larger variation across seeds.

The estimator diagnostics show a clear separation between WP and the node perturbation methods. Vanilla NP obtained the highest cosine similarity, followed closely by IS-NP, while fan-in NP was lower than both. WP had a substantially lower cosine similarity than all node perturbation methods. The variance ranking follows a similar pattern: IS-NP and vanilla NP obtained the lowest estimator variance values, fan-in NP was higher, and WP had the highest variance. The clearest distinction is therefore between WP and the node perturbation methods, but fan-in NP is also separated from IS-NP and vanilla NP on both diagnostic metrics.

The metric-wise ranking in Table~\ref{tab:california_housing_results_ranking} gives the lowest average rank to vanilla NP. Fan-in NP and IS-NP obtain the same average rank, while WP has the highest average rank. The ranking is less uniform than for the sinus task: vanilla NP ranks best on the loss, score, and cosine metrics, IS-NP ranks best on variance, and WP ranks best on convergence epoch. The clearest distinction is again between WP and the node perturbation methods for final performance and estimator diagnostics, while the ordering among the three node perturbation methods depends on the metric considered.

\subsection{MNIST Classification}

Figure~\ref{fig:mnist_results} and Table~\ref{tab:mnist_results_summary} show that all methods were able to learn meaningful representations for the MNIST classification task, but with clearly separated final performance levels. Backpropagation achieved the highest test accuracy at 97.6% and converged substantially faster than the perturbation methods. All perturbation methods converged more slowly and to higher loss levels than backpropagation, but still achieved meaningful classification performance. Among the perturbation methods, IS-NP obtained the highest test accuracy at 94.9%, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. The same ordering is visible in the test loss values, where the separation between the perturbation methods is even clearer. The reported standard deviations are small relative to the differences between methods, indicating that the ordering is consistent across seeds.

The estimator diagnostics follow the same ordering as the performance metrics. IS-NP obtained both the highest cosine similarity and the lowest estimator variance, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. WP was separated from the other perturbation methods on both diagnostic metrics, with substantially lower cosine similarity and higher variance. The gap between the node perturbation methods is also visible: fan-in NP is close to IS-NP, while vanilla NP is clearly lower in cosine similarity and higher in variance.

The metric-wise ranking in Table~\ref{tab:mnist_results_ranking} is highly consistent across metrics. IS-NP receives the best average rank and ranks first on every reported metric. Fan-in NP ranks second on all metrics, followed by vanilla NP and WP. The resulting ordering is therefore IS-NP, fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP, with only the convergence epoch producing a small deviation between vanilla NP and WP.

\subsection{CIFAR-10 Classification}

Figure~\ref{fig:cifar10_results} and Table~\ref{tab:cifar10_results_summary} show that the gap between backpropagation and the perturbation methods is larger on CIFAR-10 than on the previous tasks. The perturbation methods remain far from the backpropagation baseline and do not learn the task well, although they still achieve accuracies clearly above random guessing. IS-NP obtains the highest test accuracy among the perturbation methods at approximately 36%, compared with the 10% chance level. Backpropagation also shows signs of overfitting, with training loss continuing to decrease while test loss remains much flatter. Among the perturbation methods, the final performance follows the same ordering as for MNIST: IS-NP, fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. The reported standard deviations are small relative to the differences between the perturbation methods, indicating that this ordering is consistent across seeds.

The estimator diagnostics follow the same ordering as the performance metrics. IS-NP obtains the highest cosine similarity and the lowest estimator variance, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. WP is again clearly separated from the node perturbation methods, with substantially lower cosine similarity and higher estimator variance. Compared with the regression tasks, the differences between the three node perturbation methods are also more visible on CIFAR-10.

The metric-wise ranking in Table~\ref{tab:cifar10_results_ranking} is highly consistent across metrics. IS-NP obtains the best average rank, followed by fan-in NP, vanilla NP, and WP. This ordering appears for all reported performance and estimator-diagnostic metrics. The only exception is convergence epoch, where the ordering is reversed, with WP reaching the convergence criterion earliest and IS-NP latest. Apart from this convergence metric, the ranking gives a clear separation between all four perturbation methods.
