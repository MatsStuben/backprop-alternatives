\section{Sustainability}

This thesis relates indirectly to sustainability through its motivation of more efficient and biologically inspired learning systems. Modern artificial intelligence systems require large amounts of computation and energy, and this challenge is likely to become more important as models continue to grow in size and capability. The methods studied in this thesis are not shown to be more computationally efficient than backpropagation in standard artificial neural networks, and the empirical results do not suggest that they are currently practical replacements. However, the broader research direction is still relevant to sustainability: biological learning demonstrates that powerful, adaptive intelligence can operate with far lower energy consumption than current digital systems. By studying learning rules that could be compatible with more biologically inspired architectures, this thesis contributes modestly to long-term research on alternative AI systems that may eventually be more energy-efficient than today's approaches.
